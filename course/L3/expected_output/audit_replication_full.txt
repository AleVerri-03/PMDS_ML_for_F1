==============================================================================
CHECK 4: Is the physics-informed loss physically correct?
==============================================================================
The paper writes the constraint as   F = 0.5 * rho * V^2 * C
The definition of a force coefficient is  F = 0.5 * rho * V^2 * A * C
                                                              ^^^
The reference area A is missing. Check the units:
  0.5 * rho * V^2        -> [kg/m^3][m^2/s^2] = kg/(m s^2) = Pa
  a force               -> N = kg m/s^2
  Pa != N, so the stated equation cannot be dimensionally valid.

Constant hardcoded in the paper's loss : 0.5*1.225*50.0^2 = 1531.2500
Constant actually present in the data  : 132.9418
Ratio (paper / data)                  : 11.5182x

Solving 0.5 * rho * V^2 * A = k_true for the two unknowns:
  if A = 1.00 m^2 (unit reference area) -> V = 14.73 m/s (53 km/h)
  if V = 50 m/s (as claimed)            -> A = 0.0868 m^2
  An F1 front wing reference area is order 1 m^2, so V = 50 m/s is
  inconsistent with this data by roughly an order of magnitude.

VERDICT: the physics term penalises the network by a factor 11.5x away from
the true relation. It does not encode physics -- it encodes a wrong
constant, and it actively pulls the prediction away from the correct
answer. The reported lambda_phys = 0.1 is what keeps the damage small.

Separately: there is no call to torch.autograd.grad anywhere in main.py.
No derivative of the network is ever taken with respect to its inputs, so
no PDE residual is ever formed. By the definition in Raissi et al. (2019)
this model is not a physics-informed neural network. It is a multilayer
perceptron with an algebraic penalty term.

==============================================================================
Lag-1 autocorrelation of Cd across timesteps: 0.779904
Adjacent rows are near-duplicates of each other. A uniformly random
80/20 split therefore places, for almost every validation row, its two
immediate neighbours in the training set.

Distance (in timesteps) from each validation row to the nearest
training row:  mean 1.045   max 4   share at distance 1: 96.1%

The paper states the split was stratified to preserve the distribution
of force magnitudes. main.py line 133 calls train_test_split(indices,
test_size=0.2, random_state=42) with no `stratify` argument. The stated
protocol is not the implemented one.

Finally, main.py lines 229-233 fit a LinearRegression calibration on
y_pred_all / y_true_all -- the FULL dataset, validation rows included --
and the paper reports the resulting improvement as a result. Any metric
computed after that step is fitted on its own test set.

VERDICT: three compounding protocol errors. Even if the task were not
trivial, the reported numbers would not measure generalisation.

SUMMARY OF DEFECTS
==============================================================================
D1  Dataset is one simulation of one geometry; no design variation.
D2  Targets are the inputs divided by a constant (exact to ~1e-13).
D3  A 26-parameter linear model reaches R2 = 1.0; the network gets 0.968.
D4  Physics term omits the reference area and is wrong by ~11.5x.
D5  No autograd-based PDE residual: the model is not a PINN.
D6  Random split on autocorrelated timesteps + calibration fitted on

None of this means the research direction is wrong. Surrogate models for
aerodynamic design ARE valuable and PINNs ARE a real method. It means the
paper does not provide evidence for either. Our project supplies it.
